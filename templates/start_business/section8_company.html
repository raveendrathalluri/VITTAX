INTRODUCTION & OVERVIEW

What is a Section 8 Company?

A Section 8 Company is a non-profit organisation incorporated under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013 — a company that has charitable, educational, religious, scientific, social welfare, sports promotion, or environmental protection as its primary objective, and whose profits (if any) are applied only toward promoting these objectives rather than distributed as dividends to members.

Section 8 companies enjoy a special licence from the Central Government and are exempt from several provisions of the Companies Act (such as using "Limited" or "Private Limited" in their name — they can simply use their name with appropriate suffix). They enjoy income tax exemption under Section 11/12 of the Income Tax Act (after registration under Section 12A and 80G), making them eligible to receive tax-deductible donations.

Section 8 is the preferred structure for NGOs, foundations, and non-profit entities over a Trust or Society, because it offers greater transparency, better corporate governance, and more credibility with Indian and foreign donors, FCRA authorities, and CSR funders.

Key advantage: Profits cannot be distributed as dividends — all surplus must be reinvested toward the company's charitable objectives.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Section 8 Company (NGO / Non-Profit) in India.

What is the difference between a Section 8 Company, a Trust, and a Society?

Section 8 Company is governed by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA), offers the highest transparency and corporate credibility, and is ideal for NGOs seeking CSR funding, FCRA, or institutional grants. Trust is formed under the state Trusts Act and is best suited for small private/family charities. Society is governed under the Societies Registration Act and is typically chosen for member-run welfare associations and clubs.

Can a Section 8 Company distribute profits or dividends to its members?

No. Under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013, payment of dividends or distribution of profits to shareholders or members is strictly prohibited. All profits, surplus income, and donations must be reinvested solely into advancing the company's stated charitable and non-profit objectives.

Can a Section 8 Company pay salaries to its founders or directors?

Yes. Founding members and directors can receive reasonable remuneration and reimbursement of expenses for legitimate executive or professional services rendered, provided it is justified, approved by the Board of Directors, and clearly disclosed in the audited accounts.

How do Section 12A and 80G tax exemptions benefit an NGO?

Section 12A exempts the entire surplus income of the Section 8 company from corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act. Section 80G allows donors to claim a 50% tax deduction on donations made to the company. VITTAX assists in securing both registrations along with NITI Aayog NGO Darpan enrollment.

Is annual audit mandatory for a Section 8 Company?

Yes. All Section 8 companies must get their books of account audited annually by an independent practicing Chartered Accountant, regardless of turnover. The company must also file annual statutory returns (Form AOC-4, Form MGT-7) with the ROC/MCA and file Income Tax Return (ITR-7).
